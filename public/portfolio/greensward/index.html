Signs of a Kept Lawn

You can tell from the sidewalk.

The Checked Stripe

Passes alternate direction each week, so the turf stands upright and the pattern reads from the street.

The Knife Edge

Beds are cut to a clean ninety-degree trench, re-struck every visit, never sprayed brown.

The Clean Threshold

Not one clipping left on walk, drive, or doormat. We photograph the threshold before we leave.

The House Standard

Ten points, kept on every visit.

  1. I

    Blades sharpened after every eight hours of cutting.

  2. II

    Never more than a third of the blade's height removed in a single cut.

  3. III

    Cut height held at three to three-and-a-half inches — tall turf shades its roots and beats the weeds.

  4. IV

    Stripe direction alternated weekly, so the turf stands upright.

  5. V

    Edges re-struck every visit. Never sprayed brown.

  6. VI

    Sprinkler heads flagged and mowed around.

  7. VII

    A message the evening before; word by 7 a.m. if rain shifts the day.

  8. VIII

    Walks, drive, and threshold blown clean. The threshold photographed before we leave.

  9. IX

    Gates closed, latched, and photographed likewise.

  10. X

    If anything is not right, say so within the day. We return and re-cut it. No forms, no discussion.

Asked, Answered

The practical part.

What happens on a rain day?+

You have word by 7 a.m. and the visit moves, usually by a day. Skipped visits are never charged.

Am I signing a contract?+

No. The plan is in writing so the price cannot move; you may pause or end the keeping with a note, any time.

How does payment work?+

A card on file, charged after each visit, and a plain statement at the close of each month. No invoices to chase.

We have a dog.+

Most of our properties do. The crew texts thirty minutes ahead, so dogs are in and toys are up before we open the gate.

What do you do with the clippings?+

Mulched finely back into the turf as a rule — it feeds the lawn. Bagged and removed on request.

Do I need to be home?+

No. Several of our clients have never seen us. They tell us the lawn is how they know we came.
